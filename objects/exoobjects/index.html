The Central Stars

The exoplanets in this Science Olympiad focus on 3 stars all of about the same age:

Name Central Star Stellar Type Distance V K Age
TRAPPIST-1 2MASS J23062928-0502285 M8 V 12 pc 18.8 10.30 7.6 Gyr
LHS 1140 2MASS J00445930-1516166 M4.5 V 15 pc 14.2 8.82 >5 Gyr
Kepler-452 2MASS J19440088+4416392 G2 IV 554 pc 13.4 11.85 6 Gyr

Alternative names for the central stars are TRAPPIST-1a, LHS 1140a, and Kepler-452a, respectively.

Oddly, Kepler-452 is not included in the list of "Most Habitable Exoplanets" listed by Wikipedia; rather, Kepler-442 is listed.

Exoplanets in the "habitable zone" means their distance from the central star could theoretically allow liquid water to pool.

Why focus on low-mass stars?

Compared to massive stars:

  • Low-mass stars are more common. So the neighborhood of the Sun is more likely to be crowded with low-mass stars than high-mass stars.

  • Low-mass stars are smaller. So when a planet-sized objects eclipses a low-mass star, it hides a larger fraction of its surface.

  • Low-mass stars have lower mass, so planet-sized objects will cause a relatively larger perturbation in the star's radial velocity.
