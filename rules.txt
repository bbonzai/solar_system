---
title: Science Olympiad 2027 Rules for Solar System, Division B
---
Participants will be tested on the content below:

    I. Identify and explain:
        * surface/atmospheric features
        * processes (e.g., cryovolcanism)
        * properties of specific objects:

        1. Objects in the Solar System: 
            1a. Venus 
            1b. Mars 
            1c. Ganymede 
            1d. Europa 
            1e. Enceladus 
            1f. Titan 
            1g. Makemake 
            1h. 101955 Bennu 
            1i. 67P/Churyumov—Gerasimenko

        2. Extrasolar Systems: 
            2a. TRAPPIST-1 
            2b. Kepler-452 
            3c. LHS 1140

    II. Habitability beyond the Earth: 

        3. Conditions relevant to habitability, including:
            3a. temperature 
            3b. atmospheric composition and retention 
            3c. presence of stable liquids (surface and subsurface) 
            3c. tidal heating 
            3d. chemical disequilibrium
    
        4. Geologic and chemical evidence of habitability, including:
            4a. historical evidence of liquid water from morphology 
            4b. surface/atmospheric composition inferred spectral features 
            4c. delivery of water and organic material from small 
                Solar System bodies

    
        5.  Biology and chemistry relevant to habitability, including:
            5a. building blocks of life 
                * proteins nucleic acids, lipids, carbohydrates), 
            5b. phases/forms of water 
                *liquid
                * amorphous and crystalline ice brines
                * clathrates
            5c. extremophiles 
                * chemolithoautotrophs
                * tardigrades
    
    III. General engineering principles and facilities including:
        * science objectives
        * instrumentation
        * tradeoffs underlying spacecraft and telescope design

        6. Exoplanet detection techniques, limited to 
            6a. transits
            6b. radial velocity
            6c. microlensing, 
            6d. direct imaging.

        7. Space Missions to solar system objects
            7a. DAVINCI 
            7b. VERITAS 
            7c. Perseverance 
            7d. Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter 
            7e. MAVEN 
            7f. ESCAPADE 
            7g. Galileo
            7h. Europa Clipper 
            7i. JUICE 
            7j. Cassini-Huygens 
            7k. Dragonfly 
            7l. OSIRIS-REx 
            7m. Rosetta 

        8. Space Telescopes for observng extra-solar-system objects:
            8a. Kepler Space Telescope 
            8b. James Webb Space Telescope
            8c. Roman Space Telescope 
            8d. Habitable Worlds Observatory
    
    IV. Mathematical concepts emphasizing algebraic manipulation.  
        * rearranging/deriving equations 
        * order of magnitude estimation 
        * dimensional analysis 
        * sketching graphs 
        * proportions 

        9. Light radiation, and temperature 
            9a. Doppler shift 
            9b. flux 
            9c. inverse-square law 
            9d. albedo 
            9e. the Stefan—Boltzmann law 
            9f. Wien’s displacement law 
            9g. equilibrium temperature _

        10. Gravity orbits, and tides: 
            10a. kinetic and potential energy 
            10b. Newton’s law of gravitation 
            10c. Kepler’s laws 
            10d. escape velocity 
            10e. tidal forces

        11. Chemistry: 
            11a. ideal gas law 
            11b. kinetics (e.g. Arrhenius equation)
            11c chemical equilibrium
